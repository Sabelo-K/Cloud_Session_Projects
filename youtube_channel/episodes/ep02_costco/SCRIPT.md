# Money Mechanics, Episode 2: How Costco Makes Money (It's Not What You Buy)

**Target length:** ~4:30–5:00 (~700 words of voiceover)
**Primary keyword:** how costco makes money (89K/mo, competition 34)
**Voice:** Kokoro TTS (free, open-source), voice `bm_george`.
**Mascot:** Penny.

> **Status: LOCKED.** All figures come from Costco's FY2025 Form 10-K and earnings release unless noted (see log at end).

---

## SCENE 1: Cold open hook

**VISUAL:** A Costco-style warehouse food court. A giant price sign reads **HOT DOG + SODA $1.50**. A calendar behind it flips from 1985 to 2025, and the price never changes.

**VO:**
> This hot dog and soda have cost a dollar fifty since nineteen eighty-five. / Forty years. / Same price. / And the company selling it couldn't care less / because it's not where the money comes from.

---

## SCENE 2: Promise

**VISUAL:** Title card: MONEY MECHANICS. A warehouse turns into a machine with gears labelled "GROCERIES", "TVs" and a big hidden gear labelled "?".

**VO:**
> Costco sells everything, from rotisserie chickens to kayaks. / But selling stuff isn't really how it makes its money. / By the end of this video, you'll see the hidden gear that powers the whole warehouse.

---

## SCENE 3: Setup

**VISUAL:** Penny at the entrance. A greeter holds out a hand: "MEMBERSHIP CARD?" Penny pays **$65** and gets a card.

**VO:**
> Meet Penny. / Before she can buy a single thing, / she has to pay Costco sixty-five dollars a year, / just to walk through the door. / It feels like a cover charge for groceries. / And that cover charge / is the whole trick.

---

## SCENE 4: Reveal 1, the membership machine

**VISUAL:** Penny's card multiplies into a huge crowd of cards. Counter: **81 MILLION** paid members.

**VO:**
> Penny isn't alone. / Costco has eighty-one million paid members. / Last year, their fees added up to more than five point three billion dollars.

**VISUAL:** A bar comparison: "MEMBERSHIP FEES $5.3B" next to "TOTAL PROFIT $8.1B". The fees bar is almost two-thirds as tall.

**VO:**
> Now compare that to Costco's entire profit for the year: / about eight point one billion. / The membership fees alone are almost two-thirds the size of it. / And fees cost Costco almost nothing to collect. / Think about what that means. / Before Costco sells a single hot dog, / it has already collected billions of dollars, paid up front, / from people who are basically promising to come back. / That money is so dependable / that Costco can afford to sell everything else / on razor-thin margins.

**VISUAL:** A renewal loop arrow spins around Penny's card: **92% RENEW**.

**VO:**
> Here's the best part, for Costco. / In the US and Canada, more than ninety-two percent of members renew every year. / That's not a store. / That's a subscription.

---

## SCENE 5: Reveal 2, cheap stuff is the bait

**VISUAL:** Back at the food court: the $1.50 combo. A "WHAT IT WOULD COST WITH INFLATION" tag flips up: **$4+**.

**VO:**
> So why keep the hot dog so cheap? / Because it's bait. / Costco's own leaders have said the price isn't changing, / even though inflation should have pushed it well past four dollars.

**VISUAL:** A $4.99 rotisserie chicken glows. Penny walks to it on a winding path past a TV, a giant pack of paper towels and a kayak. Each item drops into her cart with a "pop".

**VO:**
> Same with the four ninety-nine rotisserie chicken. / Costco keeps famous prices like these low on purpose. / They make the membership feel like a no-brainer. / And on her way to grab a cheap chicken, / Penny somehow picks up a TV, / forty rolls of paper towels, / and a kayak.

**VISUAL:** Receipt unrolls: **$412.87**. Then a calculator: $65 ÷ 10% = **$650**, and a bigger number stamps in: **$3,300+ per member**.

**VO:**
> Let's do Penny's math. / She paid sixty-five dollars to get in. / If Costco saves her ten percent, / she has to spend six hundred and fifty dollars a year / just to break even. / But divide Costco's total sales by its paid members, / and it comes to more than three thousand dollars each. / Members aren't just breaking even. / They're moving their whole shopping life in.

---

## SCENE 6: Reveal 3, the upgrade

**VISUAL:** A shiny "EXECUTIVE" card appears next to Penny's: **$130 / year**.

**VO:**
> Then comes the upgrade. / The executive membership costs a hundred and thirty dollars, / double the basic fee, / and gives some of it back as rewards on what you spend. / The more you spend, the better the deal looks. / And the only way to make the upgrade pay off / is to bring even more of your shopping to Costco.

**VISUAL:** Pie: 38.7M executive members out of 81M; a second pie shows **74% OF SALES**.

**VO:**
> Almost thirty-nine million members have taken it. / And those executive members / account for nearly three quarters of everything Costco sells.

---

## SCENE 7: The so-what

**VISUAL:** The machine again. The hidden gear is revealed: **MEMBERSHIPS**. It drives all the others.

**VO:**
> So here's the machine. / Costco sells products at thin margins to keep prices low. / Low prices make the membership feel worth it. / Members renew, year after year. / And those renewals are where the real money is. / The warehouse is just the showroom / for the subscription. / Costco doesn't need you to buy any one thing. / It needs you to come back next year.

**VISUAL:** Penny with a calculator. Checklist appears.

**VO:**
> So, is a membership worth it? / It can be. / But do the math. / One: only count savings on things you'd buy anyway. / Two: bulk is only cheaper if you use it before it goes bad. / Three: if the upgrade doesn't pay for itself in rewards, / stay basic.

---

## SCENE 8: Payoff and bridge

**VISUAL:** Penny walks out with her cart. In the distance, a glittering casino sign lights up.

**VO:**
> Pawn shops sell you time. / Costco sells you belonging. / But there's one business that sells you something even stranger: / the feeling that you might win. / How casinos make money, / next on Money Mechanics.

**END SCREEN:** Watch next + subscribe. Penny waves.
