# Money Mechanics, Episode 5: How "Free" Apps Make Billions

**Upload order:** publish as the **fifth** video, since Ep. 4 (casinos) ends with "How free apps make billions, next on Money Mechanics."
**Target length:** ~4:15 (~630 words)
**Primary keyword:** how companies make money / how do free apps make money
**Voice:** Kokoro TTS, `bm_george`.
**Mascot:** Penny.

> **Status: LOCKED.** Fact-checked 2026-10-13 (see log at end).

---

## SCENE 1: Cold open hook

**VISUAL:** Penny scrolling on her phone in bed. A price tag on the app reads **$0**. Then a receipt prints out of the phone: "META 2025 REVENUE: $201 BILLION".

**VO:**
> Penny has never paid a cent for her favourite apps. / Not one. / And yet last year, / the company behind Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp / made two hundred and one billion dollars. / So if Penny isn't paying, / who is?

---

## SCENE 2: Promise

**VISUAL:** Title card: MONEY MECHANICS. The phone turns into a machine with three dials: "ATTENTION", "DATA" and "AUCTION".

**VO:**
> Last episode, the casino let Penny play for free drinks. / Free apps run a quieter version of the same deal. / Here are the three engines that turn a free app / into one of the biggest money machines on Earth.

---

## SCENE 3: Engine 1, attention

**VISUAL:** Header "ENGINE #1: ATTENTION". Penny scrolls; between every few posts, an ad card slides in. A counter labelled "ADS SEEN" ticks up.

**VO:**
> Engine number one: / attention. / Every time Penny scrolls, / a few of the posts aren't posts. / They're ads. / And advertisers pay for every one she sees. / In twenty twenty-five, / ninety-eight percent of Meta's money / came from advertising. / One hundred and ninety-six billion dollars.

**VISUAL:** A pie chart, almost entirely red: "ADS: 98%". Then a split: "MORE ADS +12%" and "PRICE PER AD +9%".

**VO:**
> So the business has two levers. / Show more ads, / or charge more for each one. / Last year, Meta pulled both. / Ad impressions were up twelve percent, / and the average price per ad was up nine. / Which means the real product isn't the app. / It's the hours Penny spends inside it. / Every extra minute she scrolls / is more inventory to sell.

**VISUAL:** A second giant: a search bar and a play button. Counter: **$82.3 BILLION** in ads, one quarter. A smaller card: "YouTube ads: $11.4B".

**VO:**
> And Meta isn't alone. / Google's parent company runs the same play / with search and YouTube. / In just the last three months of twenty twenty-five, / it made eighty-two billion dollars from ads. / More than eleven billion of that / came from YouTube alone. / Free to watch. / Very much not free to run.

---

## SCENE 4: Engine 2, data

**VISUAL:** Header "ENGINE #2: DATA". Tiny labels float off Penny into a folder marked "PENNY": "likes dogs", "searched running shoes", "lives near the beach", "up at 1 a.m.".

**VO:**
> Engine number two: / data. / An ad for running shoes / is worth more if it's shown to someone / who just searched for running shoes. / So every like, / every search, / every pause on a video / helps the app guess what Penny wants / and when she's most likely to buy it.

**VISUAL:** Two advertisers: one throws a dart blindfolded at a crowd; the other places a single arrow on Penny. The second one's price tag is higher.

**VO:**
> That's why targeted ads sell for more than random ones. / Penny isn't the customer. / The advertisers are the customers. / Penny's attention, / and everything the app learns about her, / is what's being sold.

---

## SCENE 5: Engine 3, the auction

**VISUAL:** Header "ENGINE #3: THE AUCTION". A tiny auction house appears inside Penny's phone. Bidders with paddles shout "$2!", "$3!", "$5!" for one ad slot.

**VO:**
> Engine number three: / the auction. / The price of an ad isn't fixed. / Every time a slot opens on Penny's screen, / advertisers compete for it / in an automatic auction / that runs in a fraction of a second. / The more advertisers want people like Penny, / the higher the price goes.

**VISUAL:** Earth with a counter: **3.58 BILLION** people a day. Then "$196B ÷ 3.58B ≈ $55 per person".

**VO:**
> And it runs on a staggering scale. / In December, / three point five eight billion people used Meta's apps every day. / Divide the ad money by all those people, / and each one is worth roughly fifty-five dollars a year. / Penny never sees a bill. / But somebody is paying for her.

**VISUAL:** A toggle: "WITH ADS: FREE" vs "NO ADS: €9.99/month". Penny looks at both.

**VO:**
> Here's the clearest proof / of what Penny's attention is worth. / When European regulators pushed back on tracking, / Meta started offering people in Europe / a version with no ads. / The price? / Starting at nine ninety-nine euros a month. / That's roughly what the company thinks / your attention is worth to it.

---

## SCENE 6: The so-what

**VISUAL:** The three dials spin. Penny steps back with a checklist.

**VO:**
> So here's the machine. / Attention creates the ad space. / Data makes it valuable. / And the auction squeezes out the highest price. / The app doesn't need your money. / It needs your time. / So spend it on purpose. / One: / turn off notifications you didn't ask for. / Every ping is an invitation back in. / Two: / check your ad settings, / and limit the tracking you're comfortable with. / Three: / set a daily screen-time limit, / because your hours are the product.

---

## SCENE 7: Payoff and bridge

**VISUAL:** Penny puts her phone face down. A notification lights it back up: "Your free trial ends tomorrow."

**VO:**
> Free apps sell your time. / But some apps want your money too, / every month, / forever. / The subscription trap, / next on Money Mechanics.

**END SCREEN:** Watch next + subscribe. Penny waves.
