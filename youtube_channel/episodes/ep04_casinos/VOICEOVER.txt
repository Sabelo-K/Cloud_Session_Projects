Penny walks into a casino with one hundred dollars. She never bets more than a dollar at a time. But by the time that hundred is gone, she'll have bet, on average, almost fourteen hundred dollars. And the casino never had to cheat her once.
Last episode, Costco sold Penny the feeling of belonging. Casinos sell her something stranger: the feeling that she might win. Here are the three engines that turn that feeling into seventy-eight billion dollars a year.
Engine number one: the edge. Take American roulette. The wheel has thirty-eight pockets. Thirty-six numbers, plus two green zeros. Bet a dollar on one number and win, and the casino pays you thirty-five dollars.
Sounds fair. It isn't. Fair odds would pay thirty-seven. So spin it thirty-eight times, win once, and you're still two dollars down. That's about five cents of every dollar you bet, gone, on average, forever. Every game in the building is built this way. Blackjack, craps, baccarat. The edge can be tiny, but it always points the same way.
And sometimes, it's not so tiny. Some wheels have just one green zero, and that one extra pocket nearly doubles the casino's cut, from under three cents a dollar to over five. Same wheel. Same game. Twice the edge.
Engine number two is where the real money is: slot machines. In Nevada, slots brought in about ten and a half billion dollars in twenty twenty-four. That's roughly two thirds of everything the state's casinos won. And on average, Nevada's slots kept about seven cents of every dollar played.
Seven cents sounds small. But here's the trick. When Penny wins, she doesn't walk away. She puts the winnings back in. The same money goes round and round, and the machine takes its little slice every single time. That's how one hundred dollars turns into almost fourteen hundred dollars of bets, until the casino has all of it. It's not one big loss. It's a thousand tiny ones that feel like a game.
And now, the machine doesn't even need a building. Last year, Americans lost almost seventeen billion dollars to sportsbooks, and almost eleven billion to online casino games, both growing by more than a fifth in a single year. The slot machine is now in Penny's pocket, open all night.
Engine number three: the freebies. Free drinks. Free rooms. Free buffets. Casinos hand out a lot for free. But that loyalty card Penny swipes tracks how much she bets and how long she plays. The casino knows its edge, so it can work out roughly how much she'll lose, and hand a slice of that back as gifts.
Those freebies aren't generosity. They're a rebate on her own losses, designed to keep her playing longer, and to bring her back next month.
So here's the machine. The edge makes sure the casino wins over enough bets. The churn makes sure you place enough bets. And the freebies make sure you come back to place more. The casino doesn't need you to lose big. It just needs you to keep playing. So if you gamble, treat it like a ticket to a show, not a way to make money. Three: skip the games with the biggest edge, like keno and double-zero roulette.
