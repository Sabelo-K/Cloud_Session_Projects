# Money Mechanics, Episode 6: The Subscription Trap, Explained

**Upload order:** publish as the **sixth** video, since Ep. 5 (free apps) ends with "The subscription trap, next on Money Mechanics."
**Target length:** ~4:15 (~630 words)
**Primary keyword:** subscription trap / finance explained
**Voice:** Kokoro TTS, `bm_george`.
**Mascot:** Penny.

> **Status: LOCKED.** Fact-checked 2026-10-13 (see log at end).

---

## SCENE 1: Cold open hook

**VISUAL:** Penny with a thought bubble: "$86 a month on subscriptions, tops." A bank statement unrolls to the floor. The real total: **$219**.

**VO:**
> Ask Penny how much she spends on subscriptions, / and she'll guess about eighty-six dollars a month. / In one survey, / that's exactly what people guessed. / When they actually added it up, / the real number was two hundred and nineteen. / More than twice as much.

---

## SCENE 2: Promise

**VISUAL:** Title card: MONEY MECHANICS. A calendar turns into a machine with three dials: "FREE TRIAL", "FORGET" and "FRICTION".

**VO:**
> Last episode, free apps sold Penny's time. / Subscriptions go straight for her wallet, / quietly, / every single month. / Here are the three engines that make the subscription trap work.

**VISUAL:** "$133 / month" × 12 = **$1,596 / year**. Penny's jaw drops.

**VO:**
> That gap is a hundred and thirty-three dollars a month. / Over a year, / that's almost sixteen hundred dollars / Penny didn't know she was spending. / And the companies on the other side of that gap / know exactly what they're doing.

---

## SCENE 3: Engine 1, the free trial

**VISUAL:** Header "ENGINE #1: THE FREE TRIAL". A big friendly "TRY FREE!" button. Penny taps it. A tiny line under it reads "then $14.99/month".

**VO:**
> Engine number one: / the free trial. / Free sounds harmless. / But to start it, / Penny has to type in her card details. / That's the whole trick. / The company isn't betting she'll love the product. / It's betting she'll forget the date.

**VISUAL:** A calendar page flips to day 30. A small "AUTO-RENEW" stamp lands on it. A coin drops out of Penny's wallet.

**VO:**
> When the trial ends, / it doesn't ask. / It just converts into a paid plan. / Nothing happens that Penny has to notice. / No new box arrives. / No reminder pops up. / Just one more line on a statement / most people never read.

---

## SCENE 4: Engine 2, forgetting

**VISUAL:** Header "ENGINE #2: FORGETTING". A row of app icons with small price tags: $9.99, $4.99, $14.99, $6.99, $12.99. Several fade to grey: "haven't opened in months".

**VO:**
> Engine number two: / forgetting. / Each subscription is small on its own. / Five dollars here. / Ten dollars there. / Too small to feel. / And that's the point. / In that same survey, / forty-two percent of people said they'd forgotten / they were still paying for something / they no longer used.

**VISUAL:** The grey tags slide into a bucket labelled "BREAKAGE". The bucket fills; a company building rises behind it.

**VO:**
> The industry even has a word for this kind of money: / breakage. / It's revenue from people who pay / but don't use what they paid for. / For a business, / it's almost pure profit. / A customer who forgets / costs nothing to serve.

---

## SCENE 5: Engine 3, friction

**VISUAL:** Header "ENGINE #3: FRICTION". Sign up: one big green button. Cancel: a maze of pages, "Are you sure?", "Wait! Special offer!", "Tell us why", "Confirm".

**VO:**
> Engine number three: / friction. / Signing up takes one click. / Cancelling can feel like a maze. / Are you sure? / Here's a discount. / Tell us why. / Are you really sure? / Every extra step / is another chance for Penny to give up / and keep paying.

**VISUAL:** A gavel. A card: "AMAZON: $2.5 BILLION" and the word "ILIAD" in big letters.

**VO:**
> This isn't just annoying. / In twenty twenty-five, / Amazon agreed to pay two and a half billion dollars / to settle the Federal Trade Commission's case / over how people were signed up for Prime, / and how hard it was to leave. / According to the F T C, / the cancellation process was nicknamed / the Iliad. / After a famously long war.

**VISUAL:** A rubber stamp "CLICK TO CANCEL: RULE" lands, then a second stamp "VACATED" crosses it out.

**VO:**
> Regulators tried to fix this. / In twenty twenty-four, / the F T C finalised a click-to-cancel rule: / leaving should be as easy as joining. / But in July twenty twenty-five, / a federal appeals court threw the rule out / before it took effect. / So for now, / the maze is mostly still there.

---

## SCENE 6: The so-what

**VISUAL:** The three dials spin. Penny steps back with a checklist.

**VO:**
> So here's the machine. / The free trial gets your card. / Forgetting keeps the money coming. / And friction makes leaving feel like work. / Companies make the most / from the customers who stopped paying attention. / So pay attention. / One: / when you start a free trial, / set a reminder for the day before it ends. / Two: / once a quarter, / go through your bank statement / and list every recurring charge. / Three: / if you wouldn't sign up for it today, / cancel it today.

---

## SCENE 7: Payoff and bridge

**VISUAL:** Penny cancels three subscriptions with a satisfying click. Then a checkout screen pops up: "Pay in 4. Interest-free!"

**VO:**
> Subscriptions charge you a little, / forever. / But there's a new way to buy / that splits the price into tiny pieces / and calls it free. / How buy now, pay later makes money, / next on Money Mechanics.

**END SCREEN:** Watch next + subscribe. Penny waves.
