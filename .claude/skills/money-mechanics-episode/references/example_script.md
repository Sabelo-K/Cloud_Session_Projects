# Money Mechanics, Episode 3: How Credit Card Companies Make Money Off You

**Upload order:** publish as the **second** video, since Ep. 1 ends with "Your credit card company… that's next."
**Target length:** ~4:30 (~620 words)
**Primary keyword:** how credit card companies make money (31K/mo, competition 38)
**Voice:** Kokoro TTS, `bm_george`.

> **Status: LOCKED.** Fact-checked 2026-10-13 (see log at end).

---

## SCENE 1: Cold open hook

**VISUAL:** Penny's credit card statement. A big **$5,000** balance, and a tiny line: "Minimum payment: $141.67". A timeline stretches off the screen: **19 YEARS**.

**VO:**
> Penny owes five thousand dollars on her credit card. / If she only ever pays the minimum, / she'll be paying it off for nineteen years. / And she'll hand over more than eight thousand dollars in interest / on top of the five thousand she borrowed.

---

## SCENE 2: Promise

**VISUAL:** Title card: MONEY MECHANICS. A credit card turns into a machine with three slots: "SWIPE", "INTEREST" and "FEES".

**VO:**
> Last episode, we saw how pawn shops profit from time. / Credit card companies play the same game, / just at a much bigger scale. / Here are the three ways they make money / every time that card leaves your wallet.

---

## SCENE 3: Engine 1, the swipe fee

**VISUAL:** Penny buys a $100 coffee-machine at a shop counter. As the card is tapped, a small slice ($2.35) of the $100 bill peels off and flies to a bank building labelled "CARD COMPANIES".

**VO:**
> Engine number one: / the swipe. / Every time Penny taps her card, / the shop doesn't get the full price. / A cut goes to the banks and networks behind the card. / On a typical Visa or Mastercard credit card, / that's around two to three dollars of every hundred.

**VISUAL:** The slices multiply into a mountain: **$187 BILLION** (2024).

**VO:**
> It sounds tiny. / But across the US, / merchants paid more than a hundred and eighty-seven billion dollars in card fees in a single year. / And the shop doesn't really eat that cost. / It quietly gets built into the prices everyone pays, / even people paying with cash. / That's also where your cash back and points come from. / The rewards are paid for, in part, by those swipe fees. / The card company hands you a little back, / so you swipe more, / and it takes a cut every single time.

---

## SCENE 4: Engine 2, interest

**VISUAL:** A calendar with a "GRACE PERIOD" window shaded green. Penny pays in full, and a padlock clicks shut on the interest machine.

**VO:**
> Engine number two is the big one: / interest. / Here's the secret most people miss. / If you pay your full balance every month, / most cards charge you no interest at all. / You're basically borrowing for free.

**VISUAL:** Penny pays only part. The padlock pops open; a big gauge spins to **22%**.

**VO:**
> But the moment you carry a balance, / the meter starts running. / And it doesn't tick once a year. / Most cards work out your interest every day / and add it to what you owe, / so next month, / you're paying interest on last month's interest. / The average rate for people who carry a balance / is now about twenty-two percent a year. / And plenty of people carry one. / Americans now owe about one point two six trillion dollars on their credit cards.

**VISUAL:** A pie: **94%** of interest and fees paid by people who carry balances.

**VO:**
> In fact, one government report found that cardholders who carry a balance / pay ninety-four percent of all the interest and fees. / The people paying in full / are getting their rewards / funded by everyone else.

---

## SCENE 5: The minimum-payment trap

**VISUAL:** Back to Penny's statement. A tiny "MINIMUM DUE" box glows invitingly. A treadmill appears under Penny; she runs, but the balance bar barely shrinks.

**VO:**
> Which brings us back to Penny's minimum payment. / It's designed to feel easy. / Her first one is about a hundred and forty dollars, / and more than ninety of that / is just interest. / She's running on a treadmill. / And as her balance slowly shrinks, / so does her minimum payment, / which keeps the treadmill running even longer.

**VISUAL:** Split screen. Left: "MINIMUM ONLY", 19 years, $8,100 interest. Right: "$200 / MONTH", under 3 years, $1,750 interest.

**VO:**
> Now watch what happens if she pays a flat two hundred dollars a month instead. / She's debt-free in under three years, / and pays about seventeen hundred dollars in interest / instead of eight thousand. / Same card. / Same rate. / Completely different outcome.

---

## SCENE 6: Engine 3, fees

**VISUAL:** Fee tags rain down: "LATE FEE", "ANNUAL FEE", "CASH ADVANCE", "FOREIGN TRANSACTION". The late-fee tag is the biggest.

**VO:**
> Engine number three: / fees. / Annual fees, / cash advance fees, / foreign transaction fees, / and the biggest of all: / late fees. / In one year, Americans paid around fourteen and a half billion dollars / just in credit card late fees. / And on many cards, / missing a payment can also trigger a penalty interest rate, / even higher than the normal one.

---

## SCENE 7: The so-what

**VISUAL:** The machine with all three engines running. Penny steps back with a checklist.

**VO:**
> So here's the machine. / The swipe fee pays the card company when you spend. / Interest pays it when you can't pay it back. / And fees catch you when you slip. / The best customer, for the bank, / is someone who spends a lot / and pays slowly. / So don't be that customer. / One: / pay the full statement balance every month if you can. / Two: / if you can't, pay way more than the minimum. / Three: / set up autopay, so a late fee never catches you.

---

## SCENE 8: Payoff and bridge

**VISUAL:** Penny cuts the minimum-payment box out of her statement with scissors. Then a warehouse door with a "MEMBERS ONLY" sign slides into view.

**VO:**
> Credit cards make money when you spend / and when you wait. / But there's one store that charges you / before you can spend a single dollar. / How Costco makes money, / next on Money Mechanics.

**END SCREEN:** Watch next + subscribe. Penny waves.
